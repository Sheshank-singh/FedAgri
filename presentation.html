⚖️ Understanding Client Drift & The FedProx Proximal Anchor

Why does standard FedAvg fail under severe Non-IID data, and how does the proximal term solve this?

1. FedAvg Failure Mode: Client Drift Divergence

When Client 1 only has Tomatoes and Client 2 only has Apples:

Loss = L_CE(w)
  • Client 1 optimizes weights to classify tomatoes, forgetting apple representations.
  • Client 2 optimizes weights in the opposite direction.
  • When the server averages these conflicting weights ($w_{avg} = \frac{w_1 + w_2}{2}$), the feature representations cancel out.
  • Result in our experiments: ResNet50 worst-client accuracy collapsed to 31.76%!
2. FedProx Solution: Proximal Regularization Constrained Consensus

FedProx attaches an elastic mathematical anchor to each client's local loss:

Loss = L_CE(w) + (μ / 2) * ||w - w_global||²
  • Clients still learn from their local crop images.
  • However, if local weights drift too far from $w_{global}$, the quadratic penalty explodes.
  • This restricts all clients to a shared parameter subspace.
  • Result in our experiments: ResNet50 worst-client accuracy jumped to 81.59% (+49.8% gain)!

Empirical Evidence: In our MobileNetV3 sweep under severe Non-IID ($\alpha=0.1$), $\mu=0.01$ achieved the highest Macro-F1 (95.36%) and best client consistency (worst client 90.85%, std 0.0335).

Defense Cheat Sheet: Top 5 Tough Reviewer Questions

Click any card below to reveal the exact scientific argument to speak to your reviewers.

Direct Answer: ResNet50 has 23.59M parameters and deep residual blocks. When a client trains locally on a severely skewed class partition ($\alpha=0.1$), its larger representation capacity allows it to quickly overfit to that specific subset. As a consequence, the weights of different clients diverge drastically in parameter space (client drift).

When the server performs simple weighted averaging, these diverged weights destructively interfere with each other, destroying feature representations for missing classes. MobileNetV3 (1.56M parameters) has strict parameter constraints and depthwise convolutions that limit local over-specialization.

Direct Answer: FedProx adds a proximal penalty term directly to the client's local loss:

Loss = L_CE(w) + (μ / 2) * ||w - w_global||²

This proximal term acts as an elastic anchor. As local client weights $w$ move away from the global weights $w_{global}$, the penalty increases quadratically. This restricts clients to exploring within a bounded neighborhood around the global consensus. Setting $\mu=0.01$ prevented ResNet50 from diverging, restoring worst-client accuracy by +49.8%.

Direct Answer: We enforced strict scientific controls:

  • Identical Client Partitions: Partition index mappings for each $\alpha$ were generated once using seed=42 and saved to static JSON files (e.g. partition_alpha_0.1_seed42.json). All 4 models loaded the exact same sample assignments.
  • Strict Test Isolation: 15% of the dataset (8,146 images) was sequestered into an isolated test split and never used in client partitioning, local training, or hyperparameter selection.
  • Zero Client Overlap: Each training sample was assigned to exactly one client.

Direct Answer: In real agricultural edge networks, communication overhead is the primary bottleneck. ConvNeXt-Tiny required 21.25 GB of cumulative communication across 20 rounds for a 95.20% FedProx Macro-F1. MobileNetV3 achieved 95.36% Macro-F1 while transmitting only 1.19 GB (an 18-fold bandwidth reduction).

Transmitting 21 GB per farm over rural 4G/IoT networks is economically and practically unfeasible, making MobileNetV3 the vastly superior operational architecture.
